# Customer

> **Version:** 1.0 (Draft)
> **Status:** Active
> **Owner:** Product Team

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Customer actor within Main Street.

It describes the customer's role, interactions and experience when engaging with merchant businesses operating on the Main Street platform.

From the customer's perspective, the merchant is the business they are interacting with. Main Street provides the underlying technology but remains largely invisible throughout the customer journey.

All customer-related workflows reference this document.

---

# 2. Definition

A Customer is an individual or organisation that purchases products, books services or otherwise interacts with a merchant's business.

Customers establish relationships directly with merchants, not with Main Street.

Main Street provides the digital infrastructure that enables these interactions in the background.

---

# 3. Primary Goal

The customer's goal is to discover, communicate with and transact with a merchant quickly, confidently and with minimal effort.

The experience should feel no different from interacting with a professionally designed business website or application.

---

# 4. Customer Experience Philosophy

Customers should recognise and remember the merchant—not the technology behind the merchant's business.

Main Street functions as digital commerce infrastructure in the same way a software engineering company builds and maintains business systems.

The platform exists to enable the merchant's digital presence without becoming the centre of the customer experience.

---

# 5. Customer Principles

Every customer experience shall follow these principles:

* Customers interact directly with merchants.
* Merchant branding always takes priority.
* Customers should not need to know that Main Street exists.
* Technology should remain largely invisible.
* Interactions should be fast, familiar and intuitive.
* Online and offline interactions should behave as one business.

---

# 6. Customer Identity

Customers may interact with a merchant in one of two ways.

## Guest Customer

Guest customers may:

* Browse the merchant's website.
* Place orders.
* Book appointments.
* Contact the merchant.
* Make payments.

Guest customers should not be required to create an account unless required by law or by merchant policy.

---

## Registered Customer

Merchants may optionally allow customers to create accounts.

Registration exists to improve convenience rather than create barriers.

Possible benefits include:

* Faster checkout.
* Saved contact information.
* Order history.
* Appointment history.
* Future loyalty programmes.
* Future personalised experiences.

Registration is always a merchant decision and should remain optional wherever practical.

---

# 7. Customer Interaction Channels

Customers may interact with merchants through multiple channels, including:

* Merchant website.
* Telephone.
* Walk-in visits.
* QR codes.
* Social media.
* Future messaging integrations.

Regardless of how an interaction begins, it should become part of the merchant's unified operational workflow.

Customers should experience one business rather than disconnected systems.

---

# 8. Customer Activities

Depending on the merchant's Commerce Profile, customers may:

* Browse products.
* Browse services.
* Place orders.
* Book appointments.
* Complete hybrid transactions.
* Make payments.
* Contact the merchant.
* Receive confirmations.
* Receive reminders.
* Receive order updates.
* Modify or cancel bookings where permitted.

---

# 9. Unified Customer Experience

Customers should experience one continuous relationship with the merchant.

Examples include:

* Booking online and paying in-store.
* Booking by telephone and receiving automated reminders.
* Walking into the business after placing an online order.
* Purchasing products while attending a scheduled appointment.

The method of interaction should never fragment the customer's experience.

Regardless of how interactions occur, merchants should manage everything through a unified operational system.

---

# 10. Customer Relationship with the Merchant

The customer relationship belongs entirely to the merchant.

Merchants are responsible for:

* Products.
* Services.
* Pricing.
* Customer support.
* Business policies.
* Commercial decisions.
* Customer satisfaction.

Main Street provides the technology that enables these relationships but does not participate in them.

---

# 11. Customer Relationship with Main Street

Customers are not expected to interact directly with Main Street.

Main Street operates as the software engineering platform responsible for building, operating and maintaining the merchant's digital infrastructure.

From the customer's perspective:

* They visit the merchant's website.
* They communicate with the merchant.
* They purchase from the merchant.
* They book services with the merchant.
* They receive communications from the merchant.

Main Street remains largely invisible throughout the customer journey.

Where attribution is appropriate, it should be discreet and secondary to the merchant's identity, for example:

**Powered by Main Street**

The purpose of this attribution is to acknowledge the underlying technology provider rather than establish a customer relationship with the platform.

---

# 12. Customer Privacy

Customers should provide only the information necessary to complete the requested interaction.

Personal information should be handled securely and in accordance with applicable privacy regulations.

Merchants remain responsible for the lawful use of customer information collected through their business.

---

# 13. Customer Communications

Customers receive communications from the merchant.

Examples include:

* Booking confirmations.
* Appointment reminders.
* Order confirmations.
* Payment confirmations.
* Order updates.
* Cancellation notices.

Although Main Street may technically deliver these communications, they should appear as communications from the merchant whenever possible.

---

# 14. Customer Responsibilities

Customers are responsible for:

* Providing accurate information.
* Honouring appointments where possible.
* Completing payments.
* Following merchant policies.
* Communicating changes promptly.

---

# 15. Customer Experience Principles

Every customer interaction should be:

* Simple.
* Fast.
* Professional.
* Mobile-friendly.
* Accessible.
* Secure.
* Consistent.
* Merchant-branded.

The platform should remove technological complexity rather than expose it.

---

# 16. Success Criteria

The customer experience is successful when customers:

* Easily find and trust the merchant.
* Complete purchases or bookings with minimal effort.
* Experience consistent service across all interaction channels.
* Recognise and remember the merchant's brand.
* Never need to think about the technology powering the experience.

---

# 17. Customer Statement

Customers should build relationships with merchants—not with Main Street.

Main Street exists as the invisible technology partner that designs, operates and maintains the merchant's digital commerce infrastructure.

Its greatest success is achieved when customers simply believe they are interacting with a professional, modern and reliable business.
