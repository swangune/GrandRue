# **42. Risks, Dependencies & Mitigation**

## **42.1 Purpose**

This section identifies the principal product risks, external dependencies and mitigation strategies associated with the successful delivery and operation of Main Street.

Recognising these risks early allows product, engineering and business teams to make informed decisions and prepare appropriate responses.

The objective is not to eliminate all risk, but to understand and manage it responsibly.

---

# **42.2 Product Philosophy**

Every innovative product carries uncertainty.

Main Street should proactively identify risks, minimise their impact where possible and continuously review them as the platform evolves.

Risk management is an ongoing product responsibility rather than a one-time planning activity.

---

# **42.3 Strategic Risks**

## Merchant Adoption

The greatest product risk is that local businesses may not adopt a new platform.

**Potential Causes**

* Limited awareness.
* Resistance to changing existing processes.
* Preference for familiar competitors.
* Perceived learning curve.

**Mitigation**

* Permanent Free tier.
* Extremely simple onboarding.
* AI-assisted website generation.
* Minimal setup time.
* Strong focus on measurable business value.
* Clear demonstration of increased local visibility.

---

## Customer Trust

Customers must feel confident purchasing from merchant websites.

**Potential Causes**

* New merchant websites.
* Unknown brands.
* Limited online reputation.

**Mitigation**

* Google Business Profile integration.
* Professional website design.
* Secure payment processing.
* Verified business information.
* Transparent merchant contact details.
* Customer reviews through Google.

---

## Merchant Retention

Merchants must continue seeing value after initial setup.

**Mitigation**

* Continuous AI assistance.
* Ongoing announcements.
* Marketing automation.
* Analytics.
* Customer relationship management.
* New platform capabilities over time.

---

# **42.4 Technical Dependencies**

Main Street relies on several external service providers.

Examples include:

* Google Business Profile.
* Stripe Connect.
* Supabase.
* Resend.
* AI providers.
* Domain providers.

These services extend platform capabilities but also introduce operational dependencies.

Where practical, the architecture should minimise vendor lock-in.

---

# **42.5 Third-Party Risks**

External providers may:

* Change APIs.
* Introduce pricing changes.
* Modify policies.
* Experience outages.
* Deprecate services.

**Mitigation**

* Modular integration architecture.
* Version-controlled integrations.
* Provider monitoring.
* Alternative provider evaluation where appropriate.

---

# **42.6 Regulatory Risks**

Privacy, consumer protection and digital commerce regulations continue to evolve.

Potential changes may affect:

* Data protection.
* Marketing communications.
* AI governance.
* Payment processing.
* Consumer rights.

**Mitigation**

* Regular legal review.
* Configurable compliance features.
* Flexible policy management.
* Modular legal documentation.

---

# **42.7 Artificial Intelligence Risks**

AI introduces unique challenges.

Potential risks include:

* Inaccurate content generation.
* Hallucinated information.
* Poor recommendations.
* Bias.
* Merchant over-reliance.

**Mitigation**

* Merchant review of important AI-generated content.
* Use verified business information wherever possible.
* Continuous model evaluation.
* Transparent AI usage.

---

# **42.8 Security Risks**

Potential risks include:

* Unauthorised access.
* Credential theft.
* Fraud.
* Malicious uploads.
* Data breaches.

**Mitigation**

* Strong authentication.
* Encryption.
* Continuous monitoring.
* Secure infrastructure.
* Regular security updates.

Detailed implementation belongs within the Technical Architecture Specification.

---

# **42.9 Operational Risks**

Operational risks include:

* Customer support demand.
* Merchant onboarding delays.
* Service outages.
* Subscription payment failures.

**Mitigation**

* Intelligent automation.
* Clear merchant guidance.
* Reliable third-party providers.
* Operational monitoring.
* Internal administration tools.

---

# **42.10 Product Scope Risks**

There is a risk of expanding the product beyond its core mission.

Examples include:

* Becoming a marketplace.
* Becoming a social network.
* Supporting unnecessary customisation.
* Introducing enterprise complexity for small businesses.

**Mitigation**

Every proposed feature should be evaluated against the Product Constraints & Guiding Principles before approval.

---

# **42.11 Market Risks**

Competitors may introduce similar capabilities.

Main Street's competitive advantage should remain focused on:

* Intelligent Simplicity.
* Local-first philosophy.
* AI-assisted automation.
* Professional digital presence.
* Unified business operations.

Competing on value is more sustainable than competing solely on feature count.

---

# **42.12 Business Risks**

Potential business risks include:

* Slower-than-expected revenue growth.
* High infrastructure costs.
* Low upgrade conversion.
* Advertising revenue fluctuations.

**Mitigation**

* Maintain a sustainable subscription model.
* Focus on merchant value.
* Continuously improve premium features.
* Diversify revenue streams responsibly.

---

# **42.13 Continuous Risk Review**

Risks should be reviewed throughout the product lifecycle.

Reviews should consider:

* Merchant feedback.
* Customer behaviour.
* Platform analytics.
* Regulatory developments.
* Technology changes.
* Market trends.

Risk management should evolve alongside the platform.

---

# **42.14 Risks, Dependencies & Mitigation Statement**

Understanding product risks is essential to building a resilient and sustainable platform.

By recognising strategic, operational, regulatory and technological risks early, Main Street can make informed decisions while remaining focused on its mission of empowering legitimate local businesses.

Risk management should support innovation—not prevent it—and should always prioritise merchant trust, customer confidence and long-term product sustainability.
