# **39. Product Roadmap & Future Capabilities**

**Revision:** 4 September 2026  
**Status:** Current product intent

## **39.1 Purpose**

This section defines the long-term evolution of Main Street beyond its initial release.

The roadmap is not a release schedule. It identifies product direction while preserving Main Street's capability-driven operational architecture and disciplined MVP scope.

---

## **39.2 Design Philosophy**

The Product Roadmap is founded on one principle:

> **Every new capability must solve a genuine merchant operating problem without weakening the coherence of the platform.**

Main Street should not add features merely because competitors offer them or because a technology is fashionable.

---

## **39.3 Product Evolution Principles**

Future development shall:

- preserve Intelligent Simplicity;
- maintain capability ownership;
- avoid business-type application forks;
- keep external providers replaceable;
- strengthen automation without removing merchant authority;
- improve merchant productivity;
- support sustainable business growth; and
- add complexity only when the business value justifies it.

---

## **39.4 Phase 1 — Establish the Merchant Operating Foundation**

The first phase establishes the merchant's identity, configuration and public/customer presence.

Core capabilities include:

- Merchant account and profile;
- adaptive onboarding/configuration discovery;
- generated storefront/website surface;
- Product/Service/Publication foundations;
- Business Hours and Location where relevant;
- Enquiry/contact;
- basic local discovery/SEO support; and
- mobile-first merchant operation.

Objective:

> Establish a valid merchant operating model and useful public presence with minimal technical effort.

---

## **39.5 Phase 2 — Core Business Operations**

The second phase expands the operational core around day-to-day customer and workforce activity.

Capabilities include:

- Online Ordering;
- Appointment/Booking;
- Payment;
- customer relationship context;
- staff/workforce membership and access;
- merchant dashboard;
- operational notifications;
- Inventory where applicable; and
- basic governed analytics when its semantic authority is ready.

Objective:

> Allow merchants to operate important customer-facing workflows from one coherent platform rather than through disconnected applications.

---

## **39.6 Post-MVP Workforce & Payroll Expansion**

Main Street shall expand workforce support to include **optional Payroll** after the initial MVP unless product sequencing is deliberately changed.

Expected product direction includes:

- Payroll Participant administration distinct from staff access;
- compensation/pay arrangement management;
- Pay Period and Pay Run workflows;
- approved variable/time-related payroll inputs where governed;
- provider-backed jurisdiction-specific payroll calculation;
- statutory filing integration where supported;
- payroll payout/execution integration where supported;
- payroll reconciliation/evidence;
- individual payslip/pay-document self-service; and
- accounting/analytics outputs derived from authoritative payroll evidence.

Payroll must remain separate from:

```text
Merchant Membership / staff authority
customer Payment
merchant subscription billing
legal-employment classification
scheduled/booking time
```

Main Street should initially rely on qualified payroll providers for jurisdiction-sensitive calculation, filing and compliance responsibilities rather than building a universal tax engine.

Objective:

> Let a growing local business manage workforce compensation through the same Main Street operating environment without making the merchant operate payroll technology directly.

---

## **39.7 Phase 3 — Business Growth**

The third phase focuses on customer acquisition and retention.

Capabilities may include:

- AI-assisted marketing;
- automated announcements;
- email campaigns;
- social distribution;
- customer segmentation;
- merchant-controlled promotions;
- review growth workflows; and
- advanced analytics once governed semantics exist.

Objective:

Help merchants grow while reducing administrative and marketing effort.

---

## **39.8 Phase 4 — Intelligent Automation**

This phase introduces deeper AI-assisted operational capabilities.

Examples include:

- predictive analytics;
- business coaching;
- customer-support assistance;
- scheduling recommendations;
- demand forecasting;
- inventory recommendations; and
- workforce planning suggestions.

AI remains subordinate to deterministic application authority.

Payroll-related AI may explain or prepare candidate inputs, but must not autonomously change compensation, approve a Pay Run, fabricate statutory facts or bypass payroll authorisation.

---

## **39.9 Phase 5 — Platform Ecosystem**

Future versions may introduce an ecosystem around Main Street including:

- public APIs;
- certified integrations;
- developer tooling;
- industry extensions; and
- business partner integrations.

The ecosystem should strengthen the merchant operating model rather than fragment it.

---

## **39.10 Multi-Location Businesses**

Future releases should support businesses operating multiple locations with appropriate location-scoped staff, inventory, schedules and analytics.

Payroll may also require location/cost-centre allocation, but such reporting must not determine legal payroll jurisdiction by itself.

---

## **39.11 Local Commerce Network**

Future versions may strengthen optional connections between local businesses through referrals, partnerships, community promotions and local discovery initiatives.

Main Street must not become a gatekeeping marketplace.

---

## **39.12 AI Business Assistant**

Future AI capabilities may provide proactive operational guidance such as daily summaries, revenue forecasts, staffing suggestions, inventory suggestions and seasonal planning.

The assistant remains an interaction/orchestration layer over deterministic Main Street capabilities.

---

## **39.13 International Expansion**

International expansion requires explicit localisation across areas such as:

- language;
- currency;
- payment methods;
- tax systems;
- payroll law and statutory reporting;
- provider availability;
- regulatory obligations; and
- local integrations.

Main Street must not treat one country's commercial, payroll or employment assumptions as universal semantics.

---

## **39.14 Platform Intelligence**

Future platform intelligence may include business-health indicators, customer trends, demand forecasts, workforce-planning insights and benchmarking.

Insights remain derived and advisory; they do not become authoritative business state.

---

## **39.15 Innovation Governance**

Every proposed feature should be evaluated against questions including:

- Does it solve a real merchant problem?
- Does it preserve Intelligent Simplicity?
- Does it have a clear semantic owner?
- Can external/provider complexity remain behind a bounded integration?
- Does it preserve merchant authority?
- Does it avoid creating a new universal engine unnecessarily?
- Does it belong in the current release sequence?

If these tests fail, the feature should be redesigned or deferred.

---

## **39.16 Long-Term Vision**

Main Street aims to become trusted digital operating infrastructure for local businesses.

It is not intended to become a website builder, marketplace, payment processor, payroll bureau or provider-specific integration shell.

Instead, it provides the coherent operating layer through which merchants establish, operate and evolve supported business capabilities while specialist providers fulfil bounded external responsibilities where appropriate.

---

## **39.17 Product Roadmap Statement**

Main Street should grow with the merchant.

A sole trader can begin with profile, presence and customer interaction; a larger merchant can progressively add Booking, Ordering, Inventory, staff access, Payroll and other capabilities without migrating to a different product or abandoning the capability-owned architecture.

> **Future capabilities extend the merchant operating model; they do not redefine its core.**
