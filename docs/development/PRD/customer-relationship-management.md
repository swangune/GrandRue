# **21. Customer Relationship Management (CRM)**

## **21.1 Purpose**

The Main Street Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system is designed specifically for local businesses.

Unlike traditional enterprise CRMs that require extensive manual configuration and data entry, the Main Street CRM is **interaction-driven**.

Every customer record is created and enriched automatically through genuine business interactions such as enquiries, bookings and purchases.

The merchant should never need to manually build a customer database.

The CRM builds itself.

---

# **21.2 Design Philosophy**

The CRM is founded on one principle:

> **Every customer interaction strengthens the merchant's understanding of their customers.**

Merchants should spend their time serving customers, not maintaining databases.

Every interaction should automatically improve the CRM.

---

# **21.3 Objectives**

The CRM shall:

* Automatically create customer profiles.
* Maintain complete interaction histories.
* Support repeat business.
* Enable personalised communication.
* Power AI recommendations.
* Feed business analytics.
* Require little or no manual administration.

---

# **21.4 Automatic Customer Creation**

A customer record shall be created automatically whenever a visitor:

* Places an order.
* Makes a booking.
* Sends an enquiry.
* Registers with the merchant.
* Completes a purchase.

The merchant does not manually create customer records.

---

# **21.5 Customer Identity Resolution**

The CRM should intelligently determine whether an interaction belongs to an existing customer.

Matching may use:

* Email address.
* Telephone number.
* Customer account.
* Other verified identifiers.

Duplicate customer records should be minimised through automatic matching.

---

# **21.6 Customer Profile**

Each customer profile may include:

## Identity

* Name
* Email address
* Telephone number

---

## Relationship

* First interaction date
* Most recent interaction
* Total interactions
* Customer status

Examples:

* New Customer
* Returning Customer
* Frequent Customer
* VIP Customer

---

## Purchase History

Automatically generated from completed orders.

Including:

* Products purchased
* Purchase frequency
* Average order value
* Lifetime spend

---

## Booking History

Automatically generated from completed appointments.

Including:

* Services booked
* Preferred staff member
* Appointment frequency
* Missed appointments
* Cancellations

---

## Communication History

Record of:

* Enquiries
* Emails
* Booking confirmations
* Order confirmations
* Marketing communications

---

## Preferences

Where known:

* Preferred services
* Preferred products
* Preferred appointment times
* Preferred communication method

These preferences develop naturally over time.

---

# **21.7 Customer Timeline**

Every customer profile includes a chronological activity timeline.

Examples:

* Customer created.
* First purchase.
* Booking completed.
* Announcement email opened.
* Review submitted.
* Repeat purchase.
* Marketing campaign response.

The timeline provides merchants with a complete view of the customer relationship.

---

# **21.8 Customer Segmentation**

Main Street automatically organises customers into useful groups.

Examples include:

* First-time customers.
* Returning customers.
* High-value customers.
* Inactive customers.
* Frequent buyers.
* Frequent bookers.
* Birthday month (where provided).

Merchants may use these groups for marketing and business insights.

---

# **21.9 Customer Registration**

Visitors may optionally register directly with the merchant.

Registration is not required for browsing.

Benefits include:

* Faster checkout.
* Saved details.
* Booking history.
* Order history.
* Easier repeat purchases.

The customer account belongs to the merchant's business.

Customers are not creating a Main Street account.

---

# **21.10 Privacy & Consent**

Customer information shall be collected and processed in accordance with applicable privacy laws.

Merchants are responsible for obtaining any required consent for:

* Marketing communications.
* Promotional emails.
* SMS campaigns.
* Data retention where required.

Main Street provides the infrastructure but does not assume the merchant's legal obligations.

---

# **21.11 Customer Notes**

Merchants may add private internal notes.

Examples include:

* Customer preferences.
* Accessibility requirements.
* Important reminders.
* Loyalty information.

These notes are never visible to customers.

---

# **21.12 Loyalty Indicators**

Without requiring a formal loyalty programme, the CRM should recognise customer value.

Examples include:

* Number of visits.
* Total purchases.
* Lifetime value.
* Years as a customer.

This information helps merchants identify and reward loyal customers.

---

# **21.13 Review Tracking**

Where supported, the CRM may record:

* Review request sent.
* Review completed.
* Review platform.
* Review rating.

This allows merchants to monitor review growth over time.

---

# **21.14 AI Customer Insights**

AI may analyse CRM data to identify opportunities such as:

* Customers who have not returned recently.
* Frequently purchased product combinations.
* Popular appointment times.
* Seasonal purchasing patterns.
* Customers likely to respond to promotions.

AI provides recommendations only.

The merchant decides how to act upon them.

---

# **21.15 Marketing Integration**

The CRM forms the foundation of the marketing system.

Merchants may target customer segments for:

* Promotional campaigns.
* Seasonal offers.
* New product launches.
* Service reminders.
* Holiday announcements.

Campaigns should always respect customer consent and applicable regulations.

---

# **21.16 Analytics Integration**

CRM data contributes to business intelligence including:

* Customer growth.
* Retention rates.
* Repeat purchase rates.
* Lifetime customer value.
* Customer acquisition trends.
* Returning customer percentage.

These insights should be presented in clear business language.

---

# **21.17 Merchant Experience**

The CRM should remain intentionally simple.

Merchants should not think of it as "CRM software."

Instead, they should experience it as:

* Customer history.
* Customer relationships.
* Repeat business.
* Helpful reminders.

Complex enterprise CRM terminology should be avoided throughout the interface.

---

# **21.18 Data Ownership**

Customer relationships belong to the merchant.

Main Street acts solely as the technology provider.

The platform shall not:

* Market directly to merchant customers.
* Share customer data with other merchants.
* Cross-promote competing businesses.

Each merchant's CRM remains completely isolated from every other merchant.

---

# **21.19 CRM Statement**

The Main Street CRM replaces manual customer management with an intelligent, automatically evolving customer relationship system.

Every enquiry, booking, purchase and interaction contributes to a richer understanding of the merchant's customers without creating additional administrative work.

For merchants, the CRM should feel less like software and more like having a reliable memory of every customer relationship.
