# **Part II – Users**

# **11. Target Customers**

## **11.1 Purpose**

This section defines precisely who Main Street is designed to serve.

Clear customer definition is essential to maintain product focus and prevent the platform from gradually expanding into unrelated markets.

Main Street is not intended for every business.

It is intentionally optimised for businesses whose success depends on local visibility, community trust and direct customer relationships.

---

# **11.2 Primary Customer**

The primary customer of Main Street is:

> **The owner or operator of a legitimate micro or small local business who wants a professional digital presence without becoming a technology expert.**

Main Street is built for merchants—not consumers.

Merchants purchase, configure and manage the platform.

Consumers simply interact with the merchant's website.

---

# **11.3 Who Is the Customer?**

There are three distinct participants within the Main Street ecosystem.

## Merchant

The merchant is Main Street's customer.

The merchant:

* Registers the business.
* Pays for subscriptions.
* Manages products and services.
* Publishes announcements.
* Receives bookings.
* Receives orders.
* Owns customer relationships.
* Makes business decisions.

The platform exists primarily to serve the merchant.

---

## Customer

The customer purchases products or services from the merchant.

Customers are **not** customers of Main Street.

They interact only with the merchant's website.

Customers may:

* Browse products.
* Browse services.
* Place orders.
* Make bookings.
* Contact the merchant.
* Register with the merchant.
* Read announcements.
* Share announcements.

Customers should never be required to know that Main Street powers the website.

---

## Visitor

Visitors are prospective customers.

They may simply be gathering information.

Visitors may:

* View the website.
* Read announcements.
* View products.
* View services.
* Find directions.
* View opening hours.
* Contact the business.

Registration is optional.

The experience should remain frictionless.

# **11.5 Businesses We Do Not Serve**

Main Street is intentionally selective.

The platform is not designed for:

* Pure dropshipping businesses.
* Anonymous online stores.
* Businesses without an identifiable legal operator.
* Illegal businesses.
* Businesses whose activities violate platform policies.
* Businesses without a verifiable business identity.
* Businesses that exist solely to redirect traffic elsewhere.

This ensures the integrity of the ecosystem and reinforces customer trust.

---

# **11.6 Merchant Characteristics**

Typical merchants share several characteristics.

They are:

* Passionate about their business.
* Busy.
* Time constrained.
* Cost conscious.
* Not highly technical.
* Community focused.
* Interested in growth.
* Looking for simple solutions.

Their expertise lies in serving customers rather than managing technology.

---

# **11.7 Merchant Needs**

Merchants require solutions that help them:

* Become visible online.
* Attract local customers.
* Present a professional image.
* Receive bookings and orders.
* Save time.
* Reduce administration.
* Build customer trust.
* Increase repeat business.
* Grow sustainably.

Every feature within Main Street should address one or more of these needs.

---

# **11.8 Customer Expectations**

While merchants are the paying customers, the success of the platform depends on delivering excellent experiences to their customers.

Business customers expect to:

* Find information quickly.
* Trust what they read.
* Navigate easily.
* Browse on mobile devices.
* Book or order with minimal effort.
* Contact businesses easily.
* Share useful information with others.

Main Street should enable merchants to satisfy these expectations without requiring technical expertise.

---

# **11.9 Merchant-Customer Relationship**

A foundational principle of Main Street is that the merchant owns the customer relationship.

Accordingly:

* Customers register with the merchant.
* Customer accounts belong to the merchant.
* Customer data is managed on behalf of the merchant.
* Communications originate from the merchant.
* Orders belong to the merchant.
* Bookings belong to the merchant.
* Loyalty belongs to the merchant.

Main Street supplies the infrastructure but does not seek to replace or mediate the relationship between merchants and their customers.

---

# **11.10 Invisible Platform Principle**

Throughout every customer interaction:

The merchant is visible.

Main Street is invisible.

Customers should perceive the experience as the merchant's own website and digital presence.

This principle extends across:

* Websites.
* Emails.
* Booking confirmations.
* Order confirmations.
* Customer accounts.
* Announcements.
* Receipts.
* Notifications.

The merchant's identity always remains primary.

---

# **11.11 Target Customer Statement**

Main Street exists for local businesses that want enterprise-quality digital capabilities without enterprise-level complexity.

The platform enables merchants to establish, manage and grow their digital presence while remaining almost entirely invisible to the customers they serve.
