# **26. Reviews & Reputation Management**

> **Authority note — 8 September 2026:** This PRD is lower-authority product evidence. Any Business Health interpretation, analytical claim or Business Recommendation derived from reputation evidence is governed by accepted `MS-PROT-083 v1.0`, while review/provider/customer/privacy semantics remain with their owning accepted authorities. MS-PROT-083 does not establish a universal Business Health score or cross-merchant benchmarking authority. Where wording below is less precise or conflicts, the accepted authorities govern.

## **26.1 Purpose**

The Reviews & Reputation Management system helps merchants build, monitor and maintain a trusted online reputation.

For many local businesses, customer reviews are one of the factors that may affect purchasing decisions and local search visibility.

Main Street simplifies reputation management by supporting review requests, monitoring governed reputation evidence and presenting useful insights, while ensuring reviews remain authentic and compliant with applicable platform and communication policies.

The objective is not to manipulate ratings but to help merchants consistently earn genuine customer trust.

---

# **26.2 Design Philosophy**

The Reviews & Reputation Management system is built upon one principle:

> **Excellent reputation is earned through excellent customer experiences.**

Main Street provides the tools to encourage legitimate customer feedback but never manufactures or manipulates reviews.

---

# **26.3 Objectives**

The Reviews & Reputation Management system should:

* Encourage genuine customer reviews where communication authority permits.
* Help merchants increase legitimate review participation.
* Support local search credibility.
* Help merchants monitor reputation evidence.
* Identify service-improvement opportunities through governed analytical semantics.
* Protect review integrity.
* Integrate with governed Business Intelligence without transferring source ownership.

---

# **26.4 Review Philosophy**

Main Street does **not** operate its own public review platform.

Customer reviews should remain on trusted third-party platforms where customers naturally search for local businesses.

This avoids fragmenting a merchant's online reputation.

Instead of creating another review ecosystem, Main Street strengthens the ones customers already use.

---

# **26.5 Supported Review Platforms**

Initially, Main Street may focus on:

* Google Business Profile

Future integrations may include:

* Facebook Reviews
* Trustpilot
* Yelp where supported
* Industry-specific review platforms

Support depends on official API availability, provider terms, data-use authority and accepted integration boundaries.

---

# **26.6 Automatic Review Requests**

Following eligible customer interactions, Main Street may automatically invite customers to leave a review where a separately accepted Notification/communication contract permits that effect.

Eligible source interactions may include:

* Completed appointments.
* Completed purchases/orders where completion/fulfilment semantics are established.
* Confirmed customer handover/delivery where the owning authority establishes the relevant fact.

Review invitations should never be triggered by invented completion semantics.

---

# **26.7 Intelligent Timing**

Review requests should be delivered at appropriate times under the applicable Notification and communication-authority boundaries.

Examples may include:

* After an authoritative completed appointment.
* After authoritative collection/handover.
* Following authoritative delivery/satisfaction evidence where supported.

Any future AI/statistical timing optimisation must remain bounded by accepted AI/analytical method authority and customer-communication constraints.

---

# **26.8 Delivery Channels**

Review requests may be delivered through supported channels such as:

* Email.
* SMS where supported.
* Messaging integrations where supported.

Messages should preserve the merchant/customer communication context and applicable sender identity semantics rather than manufacture Main Street as the business counterparty.

---

# **26.9 Merchant Control**

Where accepted configuration authority supports it, merchants may control matters such as:

* Whether review requests are used.
* Manual review-request initiation.
* Supported timing/frequency preferences.
* Reminder preferences within applicable communication rules.

Merchant configuration cannot override platform/provider/legal communication constraints.

---

# **26.10 Fair Review Policy**

Main Street shall never:

* Generate fake reviews.
* Purchase reviews.
* Offer misleading incentives.
* Suppress legitimate negative reviews.
* Manipulate review scores.
* Publish fabricated reviews on behalf of customers.

The platform promotes authentic customer feedback only.

---

# **26.11 Reputation Dashboard**

Merchants may have access to a simple reputation presentation where provider and analytical evidence support it.

Examples may include:

* Total observed reviews.
* Provider-reported rating evidence.
* Recent reviews.
* Defined review-growth measures.
* Defined review-request outcome measures.
* Governed reputation trends.

Presentation should distinguish provider evidence from Main Street analytical interpretation.

---

# **26.12 AI Reputation Insights**

Artificial Intelligence may assist with bounded interpretation of review text under accepted AI and data-use authority.

Potential analytical themes include recurring positive or improvement topics such as:

* Customer service.
* Waiting time.
* Appointment delay.
* Product availability.
* Communication.

AI output must not alter or misrepresent customer feedback. Material analytical propositions presented to the merchant must resolve to governed analytical artifacts where MS-PROT-083 applies.

---

# **26.13 Reputation Growth Recommendations**

The platform may present governed Business Recommendations such as considering whether to:

* Request reviews after eligible customer interactions.
* Improve business description.
* Publish relevant announcements.
* Update opening hours where inaccurate.
* Respond to customer enquiries.
* Add useful business photographs.

Recommendations remain advisory. They do not establish Merchant Intent or authorise the corresponding operation by themselves.

---

# **26.14 Review Monitoring**

Where supported by connected platforms and permitted data-use authority, Main Street may observe provider evidence such as:

* New reviews.
* Updated reviews.
* Rating changes.
* Review volume.

Material analytical interpretation may contribute to Business Insights or, through a separately accepted integration, Merchant Attention. Provider evidence does not itself define Merchant Attention.

---

# **26.15 Review Responses**

Where supported by third-party platform APIs, Main Street may allow merchants to draft or publish responses to customer reviews under the applicable provider, authorisation and communication contracts.

Artificial Intelligence may assist by suggesting response drafts.

The merchant remains responsible for the instruction to publish unless a separate future delegated authority explicitly establishes otherwise.

---

# **26.16 Review Analytics**

Review evidence may participate in Business Intelligence through governed Analytical Input Bindings and Measure Definitions.

Potential measures may include:

* Reviews observed.
* Provider-reported average rating.
* Defined review growth.
* Defined response rates.
* Defined review-request conversion measures.

Terms such as `customer satisfaction trend` require explicit analytical semantics and must not be inferred automatically from ratings/review text.

---

# **26.17 Customer Privacy**

Review requests and review analytics shall respect applicable customer communication, data-use and privacy authority.

The platform shall:

* Honour applicable communication preferences/authority.
* Avoid excessive reminders.
* Protect customer personal information.
* Avoid treating retained review/customer information as unrestricted analytical-use authority.

---

# **26.18 Business Health Integration**

Reputation evidence may contribute to one or more future **Business Health Indicators** where accepted Indicator Definitions establish the analytical meaning and sufficient governed evidence exists.

Potential underlying observations may include:

* Review volume/growth measures.
* Provider-reported rating evidence.
* Review-request response measures.
* Response-handling evidence where relevant.

Main Street does **not** currently establish a universal weighted Business Health score, and reputation evidence does not receive an arbitrary global health weight merely because it is available.

A Business Health assessment also does not automatically create Merchant Attention or an executable operation.

---

# **26.19 Future Capabilities**

Future enhancements may include:

* Multi-platform reputation aggregation under accepted source/identity semantics.
* AI-assisted theme/sentiment analysis under governed inference contracts.
* Industry-specific reputation insights where valid analytical definitions exist.
* Voice review support.
* Video testimonial management.

Cross-merchant or competitor benchmarking is not authorised by this PRD. Any future benchmarking must satisfy the separate material governance required by `MS-PROT-083-DQ-013` and any applicable provider/data-protection authority.

---

# **26.20 Reviews & Reputation Management Statement**

Reviews & Reputation Management helps merchants build customer trust by supporting authentic feedback and translating governed reputation evidence into useful operational understanding.

Rather than competing with established review platforms, Main Street should strengthen a merchant's ability to use those platforms while preserving provider evidence, customer privacy and merchant authority.

Any analytical interpretation remains derived decision support under MS-PROT-083 rather than a new reputation or Business Health source of truth.
